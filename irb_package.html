Barriers to Valid Medical Research Among Early-Career Researchers in Egypt

A Modified Delphi Study Protocol

Author

Early-career Researchers Taskforce of the Egyptian Medical Research Challenge

Round-1 Delphi Questionnaire

Study title

Barriers to Valid Medical Research Among Early-Career Researchers in Egypt: A Modified Delphi Study

Participant instructions

Thank you for taking part.

This questionnaire asks for your views on barriers that may limit the ability of early-career medical researchers in Egypt to conduct valid, relevant, and high-value research.

Please consider the Egyptian medical research environment as a whole. Your ratings should reflect the importance of each barrier for early-career researchers, rather than the performance of a particular person, department, university, hospital, or organization.

For each statement, provide two ratings:

  • Importance: How important is this barrier in limiting valid, relevant, and high-value medical research among early-career researchers in Egypt?
  • Modifiability: To what extent could this barrier realistically be changed or improved through actions at the individual, supervisory, institutional, university, health-system, or national level?

If you do not have enough knowledge or experience to rate an item, select “Cannot judge / insufficient experience.”

Please do not name individuals, supervisors, departments, institutions, hospitals, patients, or organizations in free-text comments.

Estimated completion time: 20 minutes.

  • 5 = moderately important / moderately modifiable
  • 9 = extremely important / highly modifiable

Section B: Barrier ratings

For every item below, select:

  1. Importance: 1–9 or “Cannot judge / insufficient experience.”
  2. Modifiability: 1–9 or “Cannot judge / insufficient experience.”
  3. Optional comment.

Domain 1: Research questions and evidence use

B1. Early-career researchers have limited ability to identify research questions that are important to patients, clinicians, health services, or national health priorities.

B2. Research questions are often selected without sufficient review of existing evidence, resulting in unnecessary duplication or poorly justified studies.

B3. Early-career researchers have limited skills in searching for, appraising, and synthesizing existing research evidence.

Domain 2: Research methods and statistics

B4. Early-career researchers have insufficient knowledge of study design and research methods appropriate to their questions.

B5. Early-career researchers have limited ability to develop clear, feasible, and methodologically sound research protocols.

B6. Early-career researchers have insufficient statistical literacy to plan, interpret, or critically evaluate data analyses.

B7. Access to appropriate biostatistical or methodological support is limited when it is needed.

B8. Research methods or analyses are sometimes chosen because they are familiar or convenient rather than because they are appropriate to the research question.

Domain 3: Scientific writing, reporting, and integrity

B9. Early-career researchers have insufficient skills in scientific writing, reporting, and communicating research findings.

B10. Early-career researchers have limited familiarity with reporting guidelines and research-integrity standards.

B11. Research findings are not always fully disseminated because of barriers to manuscript preparation, publication, or presentation.

B12. Academic or publication incentives may encourage quantity of outputs rather than rigor, relevance, transparency, and research value.

Domain 4: Supervision and mentorship

B13. Early-career researchers have limited access to supervisors with sufficient time to provide ongoing research support.

B14. The methodological expertise of the supervisor does not always match the needs of the research question or study design.

B15. Feedback from supervisors is sometimes delayed, insufficiently detailed, or inconsistent across the research process.

B16. Formal mentorship opportunities and access to research networks are limited for early-career researchers.

B17. Unclear authorship, academic-credit, or ownership practices can negatively affect collaboration and research quality.

Domain 5: Time and competing responsibilities

B18. Early-career researchers have insufficient protected time for research because of clinical, academic, examination, teaching, or administrative responsibilities.

B19. Competing responsibilities make it difficult for early-career researchers to complete research projects within realistic timelines.

B20. Early-career researchers have limited opportunities to build research confidence and sustained engagement because research activity is fragmented or interrupted.

Domain 6: Infrastructure, resources, and data

B21. Access to relevant, reliable, and usable clinical or administrative data is limited.

B22. Data-management systems and information infrastructure do not adequately support high-quality research.

B23. Access to laboratory facilities, technical equipment, software, or other research infrastructure is limited when needed.

B24. Access to databases, full-text articles, evidence resources, or literature-search support is insufficient.

B25. Institutional research-support services, such as protocol development, statistics, data management, writing support, or grant support, are insufficient or difficult to access.

Domain 7: Funding and administrative processes

B26. Early-career researchers have limited access to funding, seed grants, or financial support for research.

B27. Funding application requirements or administrative processes create barriers for early-career researchers.

B28. Ethics-review and institutional approval procedures are difficult to navigate, slow, inconsistent, or insufficiently supported.

B29. Administrative requirements after approval, such as documentation, reporting, procurement, or permissions, can delay or limit research.

Domain 8: Collaboration and institutional culture

B30. Opportunities for collaboration across disciplines, departments, institutions, and regions are limited.

B31. Limited access to multidisciplinary teams reduces the relevance, methodological quality, or implementation potential of research.

B32. The institutional research culture does not consistently value rigorous, relevant, transparent, and well-reported research.

B33. Institutional incentives, promotion systems, or recognition mechanisms do not adequately reward high-quality and high-value research.

B34. Research training and support are not sufficiently integrated into medical education, clinical training, and early academic career development.

Domain 9: Governance and national ecosystem

B35. Research governance structures do not consistently provide clear, efficient, and supportive pathways for early-career researchers.

B36. National or institutional research priorities are not sufficiently clear, accessible, or linked to early-career research opportunities.

B37. Opportunities for inter-institutional and international collaboration are limited.

B38. National research funding, infrastructure, policy, and career-development mechanisms are insufficient to support early-career medical researchers.

Round-2 barrier inclusion rules

The final Round-2 survey will contain only:

  • Barrier items that met the protocol’s retention rule after Round 1.

Section C: Candidate intervention ratings

Instructions

The following interventions have been developed from the protocol’s prespecified intervention domains and from the Round-1 findings. Please consider whether each intervention could meaningfully address important barriers for early-career medical researchers in Egypt.

Rate each intervention on:

  • Importance/potential impact: If implemented appropriately, how important and potentially impactful would this intervention be?
  • Feasibility: How feasible would this intervention be in the Egyptian context within a realistic timeframe and with realistic resources?

Proposed intervention items

C1. Introduce or strengthen structured curricula in research methodology, protocol development, evidence appraisal, and critical interpretation of research.

C2. Provide structured training in biostatistics, data management, and appropriate use and interpretation of statistical analyses.

C3. Provide structured training and practical support in scientific writing, reporting guidelines, research integrity, and responsible dissemination.

C4. Establish formal mentorship systems that match early-career researchers with accessible mentors or supervisors who have relevant expertise.

C5. Provide supervisor-development programmes addressing mentorship, methodological oversight, feedback practices, research integrity, authorship, and fair academic-credit practices.

C6. Establish institutional statistical and methodological consultation services that are accessible to early-career researchers.

C7. Allocate protected research time for early-career researchers within clinical, academic, and training schedules.

C8. Create small-grant, seed-funding, or protected-budget mechanisms for early-career research projects.

C9. Improve access to research infrastructure, including laboratory facilities, software, literature resources, databases, data-management support, and technical resources.

C10. Establish or strengthen institutional research-support offices to assist with protocol development, ethics applications, regulatory processes, grants, data management, and dissemination.

C11. Develop clearer and more efficient systems for access to appropriate research data, while maintaining privacy, governance, and ethical safeguards.

C12. Develop multidisciplinary and inter-institutional research collaboration networks for early-career researchers.

C13. Strengthen research-integrity policies, authorship guidance, reporting standards, and institutional mechanisms that support transparent and responsible research.

C14. Reform academic promotion, recognition, and incentive systems to reward rigor, relevance, transparency, collaboration, and complete dissemination rather than publication quantity alone.

C15. Develop transparent research-priority-setting mechanisms that involve clinicians, patients or the public where appropriate, health-system stakeholders, and researchers.

C16. Establish or strengthen national programmes for research-capacity development, early-career researcher support, research infrastructure, and equitable funding access.

C17. Create mechanisms that support dissemination, uptake, implementation, and use of research findings by health services, policymakers, educators, and other end users.
