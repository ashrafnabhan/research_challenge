Invitation to take part

You are invited to participate in a research study about barriers that may affect the ability of early-career medical researchers in Egypt to conduct valid, relevant, and high-value research.

Before deciding whether to participate, please read this information carefully. It explains why the study is being done, what participation involves, possible risks and benefits, and how your information will be handled.

Taking part is entirely voluntary.

Why is this study being conducted?

Medical research should address important questions and use appropriate methods so that its findings can be trusted and used. However, early-career researchers may face barriers related to research skills, supervision, time, funding, data access, infrastructure, ethics and administrative procedures, institutional culture, incentives, and the wider research system.

This study aims to develop expert consensus on:

  • The barriers considered most important in limiting valid, relevant, and high-value medical research among early-career researchers in Egypt.
  • Which barriers are considered modifiable.
  • Which interventions are considered important, feasible, and suitable for prioritization.

The study does not aim to measure how common each barrier is in Egypt, determine whether a particular barrier causes poor research quality, evaluate institutions or individuals, or assess the performance of any named supervisor, department, university, hospital, or organization.

Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because of your experience in one or more areas relevant to medical research in Egypt. Eligible participants may include:

  • Medical students, house officers, residents, teaching assistants, assistant lecturers, or equivalent early-career medical researchers.
  • Research supervisors.
  • Biostatisticians, epidemiologists, research methodologists, evidence-synthesis specialists, qualitative researchers, or related experts.
  • Members of ethics committees or research-governance structures.

You should participate only if you have sufficient familiarity with the Egyptian medical research environment to provide informed judgments.

What will happen if I take part?

This is a two-round online modified Delphi study.

Round 1

If you agree to participate:

  • You will complete an electronic consent form.
  • You will be asked a small number of background questions, such as your stakeholder category, career stage, type of institution, and research-related experience.
  • You will rate a series of candidate barriers on two 9-point scales:
    • Importance: from 1 = not important at all to 9 = extremely important.
    • Modifiability: from 1 = not modifiable to 9 = highly modifiable.
  • You may select “cannot judge / insufficient experience” for any item you do not feel able to rate.
  • You may provide optional comments or suggest additional barriers.

Round 1 is expected to take approximately 20 minutes.

Round 2

Participants who complete Round 1 may be invited to take part in Round 2.

In Round 2:

  • You will receive anonymized group-level feedback from Round 1, such as the number of valid responses, median rating, interquartile range, and proportion of participants giving high ratings.
  • You will be asked to reconsider and re-rate retained or revised barrier items.
  • You will rate candidate interventions according to:
    • Importance/potential impact: from 1 = not important/no meaningful impact to 9 = extremely important/very high potential impact.
    • Feasibility: from 1 = not feasible to 9 = highly feasible.
  • You may again select “cannot judge / insufficient experience” and provide optional comments.

Round 2 is expected to take approximately 15 minutes.

There will be no face-to-face group discussion. You will not be asked to interact with other participants.

What should I consider when responding?

Please consider the Egyptian medical research system as a whole. The purpose is not to assign blame to individual researchers, supervisors, departments, universities, hospitals, ethics committees, or funding bodies.

Please do not include:

  • Names of colleagues, supervisors, students, institutions, departments, hospitals, or organizations.
  • Confidential organizational information.
  • Allegations about named individuals.
  • Information that could identify you or another person.

If identifying information is entered into a free-text comment, the research team may remove or redact it before analysis, feedback, or reporting.

Is participation voluntary?

Yes. Participation is completely voluntary.

You may decline to participate without giving a reason. If you choose not to participate, there will be no penalty and no effect on your academic standing, supervision, assessment, employment, promotion, professional relationships, access to services, or future opportunities.

This is particularly important for junior participants: your supervisor, department, institution, or university will not be informed of whether you accepted or declined the invitation, except where they already know through their own independent means.

You may skip any optional question. However, you must provide enough required ratings for your questionnaire to be considered complete according to the study’s prespecified completion rules.

Can I withdraw?

You may stop the survey at any time before submitting it, without giving a reason.

After submitting a survey, you may request withdrawal of your data by contacting the research team at info@ecebm.com. Your data can be removed only while it remains identifiable through the study linkage system and before the relevant dataset has been de-identified, locked, or incorporated into aggregate analysis.

Once data have been de-identified and combined into aggregate analyses, it may no longer be possible to identify and remove your individual responses.

What are the possible risks?

This study is expected to involve no more than minimal risk.

Possible risks include:

  • Minimal discomfort when considering barriers related to supervision, institutional support, research governance, or research culture.
  • Concern that professional or institutional views could be recognized.

To reduce these risks:

  • Participation will occur through an electronic survey rather than a group discussion.
  • Individual ratings will not be shown to other participants.
  • Feedback between rounds will be presented only in aggregate form.
  • Identifying contact information will be stored separately from survey responses.
  • The analysis dataset will use a study identifier rather than your name or email address.
  • Reports will present aggregated findings and will avoid reporting small cells or details that could identify an individual or institution.
  • Free-text comments will be screened for identifying information before they are used in group feedback or dissemination.

Your responses are confidential, but they are not completely anonymous to the limited research staff responsible for consent administration, round linkage, and secure data management.

Are there benefits to taking part?

You may not receive a direct personal benefit from participation.

Your contribution may help identify priorities for improving research education, supervision, methodological support, infrastructure, governance, funding, incentives, and collaboration for early-career medical researchers in Egypt.

Will I be paid or reimbursed?

You will not receive payment. Participants who complete both rounds may receive a certificate of participation, if requested. The certificate will not include your ratings or comments.

How will my information be kept confidential?

The study will collect limited contact information, such as your name and email address, only to send invitations, reminders, and the Round-2 link.

Your contact information will be stored separately from survey responses. A secure linkage file will connect your contact details with a unique study identifier so that the research team can invite Round-1 participants to Round 2 and process a withdrawal request before de-identification.

Only designated members of the research team will have access to the contact and linkage file. Steering-group members, supervisors, institutional leaders, and other participants will not have access to identifiable individual responses.

Survey data will be stored on [name of secure survey platform] and exported to secure, password-protected, access-controlled institutional storage at [institution]. Data will be encrypted in transit and, where supported by the platform and institutional systems, at rest.

The study team will not intentionally collect patient information, sensitive health information, or names of colleagues or institutions in open-text comments.

How long will data be kept?

Identifiable contact and linkage data will be retained until they are no longer required for Round-2 invitation, withdrawal management, and data verification, after which they will be securely destroyed.

De-identified research data, consent records, study documents, and the audit trail will be retained for the period required by MTI institutional policy and ethical requirements.

Will my information be shared?

The study may result in publications, conference presentations, policy briefs, workshops, webinars, and an open research-capacity toolkit.

Results will be reported in aggregate form. No individual participant, named supervisor, department, institution, or organization will be identified without explicit separate permission.

Subject to ethics approval, participant consent, institutional policy, and safeguards against deductive identification, the study team may share:

  • Aggregated study findings.
  • The final questionnaire.
  • Item-development documentation.
  • De-identified numerical data.
  • Analytic code.

The following will not be publicly shared:

  • Names, email addresses, and contact information.
  • Consent records.
  • The linkage file.
  • Raw identifiable comments.
  • Information that could reasonably identify an individual, institution, department, or organization.

This study has been reviewed by Institutional Review Board, Faculty of Medicine, MTI University

Section B: Barrier ratings

For every item below, select:

  1. Importance: 1–9 or “Cannot judge / insufficient experience.”
  2. Modifiability: 1–9 or “Cannot judge / insufficient experience.”
  3. Optional comment.

Domain 1: Research questions and evidence use

B1. Early-career researchers have limited ability to identify research questions that are important to patients, clinicians, health services, or national health priorities.

B2. Research questions are often selected without sufficient review of existing evidence, resulting in unnecessary duplication or poorly justified studies.

B3. Early-career researchers have limited skills in searching for, appraising, and synthesizing existing research evidence.

Domain 2: Research methods and statistics

B4. Early-career researchers have insufficient knowledge of study design and research methods appropriate to their questions.

B5. Early-career researchers have limited ability to develop clear, feasible, and methodologically sound research protocols.

B6. Early-career researchers have insufficient statistical literacy to plan, interpret, or critically evaluate data analyses.

B7. Access to appropriate biostatistical or methodological support is limited when it is needed.

B8. Research methods or analyses are sometimes chosen because they are familiar or convenient rather than because they are appropriate to the research question.

Domain 3: Scientific writing, reporting, and integrity

B9. Early-career researchers have insufficient skills in scientific writing, reporting, and communicating research findings.

B10. Early-career researchers have limited familiarity with reporting guidelines and research-integrity standards.

B11. Research findings are not always fully disseminated because of barriers to manuscript preparation, publication, or presentation.

B12. Academic or publication incentives may encourage quantity of outputs rather than rigor, relevance, transparency, and research value.

Domain 4: Supervision and mentorship

B13. Early-career researchers have limited access to supervisors with sufficient time to provide ongoing research support.

B14. The methodological expertise of the supervisor does not always match the needs of the research question or study design.

B15. Feedback from supervisors is sometimes delayed, insufficiently detailed, or inconsistent across the research process.

B16. Formal mentorship opportunities and access to research networks are limited for early-career researchers.

B17. Unclear authorship, academic-credit, or ownership practices can negatively affect collaboration and research quality.

Domain 5: Time and competing responsibilities

B18. Early-career researchers have insufficient protected time for research because of clinical, academic, examination, teaching, or administrative responsibilities.

B19. Competing responsibilities make it difficult for early-career researchers to complete research projects within realistic timelines.

B20. Early-career researchers have limited opportunities to build research confidence and sustained engagement because research activity is fragmented or interrupted.

Domain 6: Infrastructure, resources, and data

B21. Access to relevant, reliable, and usable clinical or administrative data is limited.

B22. Data-management systems and information infrastructure do not adequately support high-quality research.

B23. Access to laboratory facilities, technical equipment, software, or other research infrastructure is limited when needed.

B24. Access to databases, full-text articles, evidence resources, or literature-search support is insufficient.

B25. Institutional research-support services, such as protocol development, statistics, data management, writing support, or grant support, are insufficient or difficult to access.

Domain 7: Funding and administrative processes

B26. Early-career researchers have limited access to funding, seed grants, or financial support for research.

B27. Funding application requirements or administrative processes create barriers for early-career researchers.

B28. Ethics-review and institutional approval procedures are difficult to navigate, slow, inconsistent, or insufficiently supported.

B29. Administrative requirements after approval, such as documentation, reporting, procurement, or permissions, can delay or limit research.

Domain 8: Collaboration and institutional culture

B30. Opportunities for collaboration across disciplines, departments, institutions, and regions are limited.

B31. Limited access to multidisciplinary teams reduces the relevance, methodological quality, or implementation potential of research.

B32. The institutional research culture does not consistently value rigorous, relevant, transparent, and well-reported research.

B33. Institutional incentives, promotion systems, or recognition mechanisms do not adequately reward high-quality and high-value research.

B34. Research training and support are not sufficiently integrated into medical education, clinical training, and early academic career development.

Domain 9: Governance and national ecosystem

B35. Research governance structures do not consistently provide clear, efficient, and supportive pathways for early-career researchers.

B36. National or institutional research priorities are not sufficiently clear, accessible, or linked to early-career research opportunities.

B37. Opportunities for inter-institutional and international collaboration are limited.

B38. National research funding, infrastructure, policy, and career-development mechanisms are insufficient to support early-career medical researchers.

Section C: Candidate intervention ratings

Instructions

The following interventions have been developed from the protocol’s prespecified intervention domains and from the Round-1 findings. Please consider whether each intervention could meaningfully address important barriers for early-career medical researchers in Egypt.

Rate each intervention on:

  • Importance/potential impact: If implemented appropriately, how important and potentially impactful would this intervention be?
  • Feasibility: How feasible would this intervention be in the Egyptian context within a realistic timeframe and with realistic resources?

Proposed intervention items

C1. Introduce or strengthen structured curricula in research methodology, protocol development, evidence appraisal, and critical interpretation of research.

C2. Provide structured training in biostatistics, data management, and appropriate use and interpretation of statistical analyses.

C3. Provide structured training and practical support in scientific writing, reporting guidelines, research integrity, and responsible dissemination.

C4. Establish formal mentorship systems that match early-career researchers with accessible mentors or supervisors who have relevant expertise.

C5. Provide supervisor-development programmes addressing mentorship, methodological oversight, feedback practices, research integrity, authorship, and fair academic-credit practices.

C6. Establish institutional statistical and methodological consultation services that are accessible to early-career researchers.

C7. Allocate protected research time for early-career researchers within clinical, academic, and training schedules.

C8. Create small-grant, seed-funding, or protected-budget mechanisms for early-career research projects.

C9. Improve access to research infrastructure, including laboratory facilities, software, literature resources, databases, data-management support, and technical resources.

C10. Establish or strengthen institutional research-support offices to assist with protocol development, ethics applications, regulatory processes, grants, data management, and dissemination.

C11. Develop clearer and more efficient systems for access to appropriate research data, while maintaining privacy, governance, and ethical safeguards.

C12. Develop multidisciplinary and inter-institutional research collaboration networks for early-career researchers.

C13. Strengthen research-integrity policies, authorship guidance, reporting standards, and institutional mechanisms that support transparent and responsible research.

C14. Reform academic promotion, recognition, and incentive systems to reward rigor, relevance, transparency, collaboration, and complete dissemination rather than publication quantity alone.

C15. Develop transparent research-priority-setting mechanisms that involve clinicians, patients or the public where appropriate, health-system stakeholders, and researchers.

C16. Establish or strengthen national programmes for research-capacity development, early-career researcher support, research infrastructure, and equitable funding access.

C17. Create mechanisms that support dissemination, uptake, implementation, and use of research findings by health services, policymakers, educators, and other end users.

Subject: Invitation to participate: Delphi study on barriers to high-value medical research in Egypt

Dear Dr/Prof/Mr/Ms [Surname],

You are invited to participate in a two-round online modified Delphi study titled: “Barriers to Valid Medical Research Among Early-Career Researchers in Egypt.”

The study aims to identify and prioritize barriers that may limit early-career medical researchers in Egypt from conducting valid, relevant, and high-value research, and to identify feasible interventions to address these barriers.

We are inviting stakeholders with experience in medical research in Egypt, including early-career researchers, supervisors, methodologists and biostatisticians, and ethics and research-governance professionals.

If you decide to participate, you will complete an online Round-1 questionnaire that is expected to take approximately 20 minutes. Participants who complete Round 1 may be invited to complete a Round-2 questionnaire after receiving anonymized group-level feedback.

Participation is voluntary. Your decision to participate or not participate will have no effect on your academic standing, employment, supervision, professional relationships, or access to opportunities. Individual responses will be treated as confidential and will not be shared with other participants, supervisors, institutional leaders, or steering-group members in identifiable form.

Please read the Participant Information Sheet before deciding whether to participate.
